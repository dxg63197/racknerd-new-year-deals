## Five things the price tag doesn't tell you

**1. It's unmanaged.** RackNerd states this plainly in its FAQ. You get root access and the ctrl panel; they run the platform. Patching, hardening, backups, application tuning and everything above the hypervisor are yours. If you want a host to manage the OS for you, this is the wrong product, and no discount fixes that.

**2. Renewal is the same price.** The specials page says the plans are lifetime recurring and that you renew "at the same rate for as long as you decide to keep the service." That's the strongest argument for these annual plans over any host running a cheap first-year, expensive-second-year promo.

**3. There are no refunds.** RackNerd's knowledge base answers the money-back question directly: no refunds and no money-back guarantee under the terms of service. Cancellation doesn't return what you paid. Buy the smallest plan that could plausibly do the job, and size up later rather than overbuying on a guess.

**4. Upgrades exist and they're quick.** You can move to the next plan up later; RackNerd's FAQ describes about a minute of downtime for the reboot. The ctrl panel handles reinstalls and OS changes at any time, and a custom ISO is available by opening a ticket.

**5. The platform details are fixed, not per-plan.** One IPv4 address, a 1 Gbps port, instant provisioning, and a choice of CentOS, Rocky Linux, AlmaLinux, Fedora, Debian or Ubuntu at checkout. IPv6 is available on request in some locations; the promo FAQ lists up to 100 free addresses in Los Angeles and France, claimed by ticket after ordering. Multiple datacenters are offered on every plan, so pick the one closest to your users at checkout rather than defaulting.

The ctrl panel itself covers the day-to-day: power on and off, reboots, OS reinstalls, an HTML5 VNC console for recovery and custom installs, inbound firewall rules, root password resets, plan upgrades, and the one-click app catalog. It's a browser panel, so nothing needs installing to get a broken server back.
